\begin{abstract}
Local deformation conditions often appear as projections of one nonlinear
equation.  Their separate vanishing need not kill the common curvature.  We
construct the common carrier for a semicosimplicial diagram as a completed
Thom--Whitney differential graded Lie algebra and prove its full obstruction
tower.  Strict completeness identifies compatible Maurer--Cartan
representatives with a total solution.  Gauge classes obey a different limit
theorem: a generally nonabelian $\varprojlim^1$ of derived stabilizers measures
their ambiguity.  A second Milnor sequence controls compatible primitives in
inverse systems of complexes.  We also give the filtered $L_\infty$ form, the
convolution carrier for comparison morphisms, and sharp examples showing why
completeness, separation, additive filtration, flatness, and totalization
hypotheses cannot be omitted.
\end{abstract}

\section{A curvature invisible in every projected equation}

Let $\Bbbk$ be a field of characteristic zero.  We use cohomological degrees:
$d$ has degree $1$, and the bracket satisfies
\[
 [u,v]=-(-1)^{|u||v|}[v,u],
 \qquad
 d[u,v]=[du,v]+(-1)^{|u|}[u,dv].
\]
For an element $a$ of degree $1$, write
\[
 \Curv(a)=da+\frac12[a,a].
\]

\begin{example}[The first missing equation]\label{ex:first}
Let
\[
 \mathfrak g^1=\Bbbk x,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak g^2=\Bbbk z,
 \qquad
 d=0,
 \qquad
 [x,x]=2z,
\]
with $z$ central.  Give $\mathfrak g$ the filtration
\[
 F^1\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g,
 \qquad
 F^2\mathfrak g=\Bbbk z,
 \qquad
 F^3\mathfrak g=0.
\]
Then
\[
 \Curv(x)=z.
\]
Thus $x$ is Maurer--Cartan modulo $F^2\mathfrak g$, but it does not lift
modulo $F^3\mathfrak g$.  Moreover, the projection
\[
 p:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow \mathfrak g/F^2\mathfrak g
\]
sends $x$ to a Maurer--Cartan element and sends its curvature to zero.  A
family of such projections can therefore erase a nonzero common obstruction.
The obstruction is not a list of projected classes: here it is the single
class
\[
 [z]\in H^2(F^2\mathfrak g/F^3\mathfrak g)=\Bbbk z.
\]
\end{example}

This calculation dictates the order of the construction.  One first needs a
single filtered carrier in which all operations and comparisons coexist.
Only then does it make sense to form its curvature, take its associated
graded class, and compare that class with the equations visible in individual
targets.

\section{The finite obstruction step}

\begin{definition}\label{def:filtered-dgla}
An \emph{additively filtered dg Lie algebra} is an uncurved dg Lie algebra
$(\mathfrak g,d,[-,-])$ with a descending filtration by subcomplexes
\[
 \mathfrak g=F^1\mathfrak g\supset F^2\mathfrak g\supset\cdots
\]
such that
\[
 [F^p\mathfrak g,F^q\mathfrak g]\subset F^{p+q}\mathfrak g.
\]
It is \emph{strictly complete and separated} when the canonical map
\[
 \mathfrak g\longrightarrow
 \varprojlim_r\mathfrak g/F^r\mathfrak g
 \tag{2.1}\label{eq:strict-completion}
\]
is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces, hence of complexes.
We abbreviate
\[
 \mathfrak g_r=\mathfrak g/F^{r+1}\mathfrak g,
 \qquad
 A_r=F^r\mathfrak g/F^{r+1}\mathfrak g.
\]
\end{definition}

The extension
\[
 0\longrightarrow A_r\longrightarrow \mathfrak g_r
 \longrightarrow \mathfrak g_{r-1}\longrightarrow0
 \tag{2.2}\label{eq:central-extension}
\]
is central and has square-zero bracket on its kernel.  This elementary fact is
the entire linearization mechanism.

\begin{theorem}[One-stage obstruction and relative lifts]
\label{thm:one-stage}
Let $r\geq1$, and let $a_{r-1}\in\MC(\mathfrak g_{r-1})$.  For any graded
lift $\widetilde a\in\mathfrak g_r^1$, its curvature belongs to $A_r^2$ and
determines a lift-independent class
\[
 o_r(a_{r-1})=[\Curv(\widetilde a)]
 \in H^2(A_r,d).
 \tag{2.3}\label{eq:obstruction-class}
\]
The element $a_{r-1}$ lifts to $\MC(\mathfrak g_r)$ if and only if
$o_r(a_{r-1})=0$.

If lifts exist, their set is a torsor under $Z^1(A_r,d)$.  After quotienting
only by gauge transformations that reduce to the identity on
$\mathfrak g_{r-1}$, the set of lifts is a torsor under $H^1(A_r,d)$, and
the relative stabilizer is $Z^0(A_r,d)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The twisted operator $d_{\widetilde a}=d+[\widetilde a,-]$ induces $d$ on
$A_r$, because
\[
 [F^1\mathfrak g,F^r\mathfrak g]\subset F^{r+1}\mathfrak g.
\]
The Bianchi identity
\[
 d_{\widetilde a}\Curv(\widetilde a)=0
 \tag{2.4}\label{eq:bianchi}
\]
therefore makes the curvature a cocycle in $A_r$.  A second lift has the form
$\widetilde a+\beta$ with $\beta\in A_r^1$, and centrality in
\eqref{eq:central-extension} gives the exact identity in $\mathfrak g_r$
\[
 \Curv(\widetilde a+\beta)=\Curv(\widetilde a)+d\beta.
 \tag{2.5}\label{eq:linear-correction}
\]
This proves independence and the lifting criterion.  Once a lift is fixed,
all others differ from it by $Z^1(A_r)$.

The relative gauge group is $\exp(A_r^0)$.  Since $A_r$ is central in
$\mathfrak g_r$, its action is
\[
 \widetilde a\longmapsto\widetilde a-d\xi,
 \qquad \xi\in A_r^0.
\]
Taking relative orbits and stabilizers gives $H^1(A_r)$ and $Z^0(A_r)$.
\end{proof}

Quotienting by all gauge transformations lying above a gauge orbit in
$\mathfrak g_{r-1}$ is different: the stabilizer of the base element can act
on $H^1(A_r)$.  The torsor assertion in Theorem~\ref{thm:one-stage} is
deliberately relative.

For a strictly complete filtered dg Lie algebra the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff
series converges on $F^1\mathfrak g^0$.  With our signs the gauge action is
\[
 e^\xi\cdot a
 =e^{\operatorname{ad}\xi}a
 -\frac{e^{\operatorname{ad}\xi}-1}{\operatorname{ad}\xi}(d\xi)
 =a-\sum_{n\geq0}
 \frac{(\operatorname{ad}\xi)^n}{(n+1)!}\,d_a\xi .
 \tag{2.6}\label{eq:gauge-action}
\]
Its infinitesimal vector is $-d_a\xi$, and
\[
 \Curv(e^\xi\cdot a)
 =e^{\operatorname{ad}\xi}\Curv(a).
 \tag{2.7}\label{eq:gauge-curvature}
\]
The series converge because every additional bracket with $\xi\in F^1$
moves a term at least one step deeper.  Equation~\eqref{eq:gauge-curvature}
also shows directly that \eqref{eq:obstruction-class} is gauge invariant.

\begin{corollary}[The recursive curvature equation]\label{cor:weights}
Suppose that the filtration is split by a complete weight decomposition
\[
 \mathfrak g=\prod_{p\geq1}\mathfrak g^{(p)},
 \qquad
 F^r\mathfrak g=\prod_{p\geq r}\mathfrak g^{(p)},
\]
and that $d\mathfrak g^{(p)}\subset\mathfrak g^{(p)}$ and
$[\mathfrak g^{(i)},\mathfrak g^{(j)}]
\subset\mathfrak g^{(i+j)}$.  Write
\[
 a=\sum_{p\geq1}a^{(p)},
 \qquad a^{(p)}\in \mathfrak g^{(p),1}.
\]
Then its weight-$r$ Maurer--Cartan equation is
\[
 da^{(r)}+\frac12
 \sum_{i+j=r}[a^{(i)},a^{(j)}]=0.
 \tag{2.8}\label{eq:weight-equation-dgla}
\]
Consequently the first unknown correction is governed by the class of the
known quadratic term in $H^2(\gr_F^r\mathfrak g,d)$.
\end{corollary}

\section{The completed Thom--Whitney carrier}

Let $\mathfrak g^\bullet$ be a semicosimplicial additively filtered dg Lie
algebra.  Thus there are filtration-preserving coface maps
\[
 \partial^i:\mathfrak g^{q-1}\longrightarrow\mathfrak g^q
 \quad (0\leq i\leq q)
\]
satisfying the semicosimplicial identities, and each $\mathfrak g^q$ is
strictly complete and separated.  Put
\[
 \Omega_q=
 \frac{\Bbbk[t_0,\ldots,t_q,dt_0,\ldots,dt_q]}
 {\bigl(\sum_i t_i-1,\sum_i dt_i\bigr)},
 \qquad |t_i|=0,\quad |dt_i|=1,
\]
with $d_{\mathrm{dR}}t_i=dt_i$.  The pullback to the $i$th face is denoted
$\delta_i^*:\Omega_q\to\Omega_{q-1}$.  Define the completed tensor product
by
\[
 \Omega_q\widehat\otimes\mathfrak g^q
 =\varprojlim_r
 \bigl(\Omega_q\otimes(\mathfrak g^q/F^r\mathfrak g^q)\bigr).
 \tag{3.1}\label{eq:completed-tensor}
\]

\begin{definition}[Completed Thom--Whitney end]\label{def:tw}
The completed Thom--Whitney totalization is
\[
 \widehat\TW(\mathfrak g^\bullet)=
 \left\{
 (x_q)_{q\geq0}\in
 \prod_{q\geq0}\Omega_q\widehat\otimes\mathfrak g^q
 \ \middle|
 (\delta_i^*\otimes1)x_q=(1\otimes\partial^i)x_{q-1}
 \right\}.
 \tag{3.2}\label{eq:tw-end}
\]
On homogeneous tensors its differential and bracket are
\begin{align}
 D(\omega\otimes x)
 &=d_{\mathrm{dR}}\omega\otimes x
   +(-1)^{|\omega|}\omega\otimes dx,
 \tag{3.3}\label{eq:tw-differential}\\
 [\omega\otimes x,\eta\otimes y]
 &=(-1)^{|x||\eta|}\omega\eta\otimes[x,y].
 \tag{3.4}\label{eq:tw-bracket}
\end{align}
Its filtration is componentwise:
\[
 F^r\widehat\TW=
 \widehat\TW\cap
 \prod_q\ker\!\left(
 \Omega_q\widehat\otimes\mathfrak g^q
 \longrightarrow
 \Omega_q\otimes(\mathfrak g^q/F^r\mathfrak g^q)
 \right).
 \tag{3.5}\label{eq:tw-filtration}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The tensor-product sign]\label{ex:tensor-sign}
Let $\mathfrak a$ be abelian with $x\in\mathfrak a^0$,
$y\in\mathfrak a^1$, $dx=y\neq0$, and $dy=0$.  If the second summand in
\eqref{eq:tw-differential} were given a plus sign independently of
$|\omega|$, then on $\Omega_1\otimes\mathfrak a$ the resulting operator
$D_+$ would satisfy
\[
 D_+^2(t_0\otimes x)=2\,dt_0\otimes y\neq0.
\]
Thus the factor $(-1)^{|\omega|}$ is necessary even before imposing the face
equations.
\end{example}

The signs in \eqref{eq:tw-differential}--\eqref{eq:tw-bracket} are forced by
the tensor product convention.  In particular, replacing
\eqref{eq:tw-end} by an ordinary normalized \v Cech total complex and
appending a symbol for the \v Cech differential does not produce a dg Lie
bracket.  The normalized total complex instead receives transferred
$L_\infty$ operations from the Thom--Whitney model
\cite{FiorenzaManettiMartinengo}.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness of the total carrier]\label{prop:tw-complete}
The formulas above make
$T=\widehat\TW(\mathfrak g^\bullet)$ a strictly complete and separated
additively filtered dg Lie algebra.  For
\[
 T_r=\TW(\mathfrak g^\bullet/F^r\mathfrak g^\bullet),
\]
the canonical map $T/F^rT\to T_r$ is injective.  Define
\[
 I_r=\operatorname{im}(T\longrightarrow T_r).
\]
Thus $I_r$ consists exactly of the finite-stage elements that admit one
coherent lift through all deeper stages.  One has
\[
 T\cong\varprojlim_r T_r\cong\varprojlim_r I_r.
 \tag{3.6}\label{eq:stable-image-limit}
\]
If every projection $T\to T_r$ is surjective, then $I_r=T_r$ and
$T/F^rT\cong T_r$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The product in \eqref{eq:tw-end} is complete.  The face equations cut out the
kernel of a continuous linear map to a separated product, hence a closed
complete subspace.  Equations \eqref{eq:tw-differential} and
\eqref{eq:tw-bracket} preserve the face equations.  Their componentwise
form gives
\[
 D(F^rT)\subset F^rT,
 \qquad
 [F^pT,F^qT]\subset F^{p+q}T.
\]
Inverse limits commute with products and equalizers, so the definition also
gives $T\cong\varprojlim_rT_r$.
The kernel of $T\to T_r$ is exactly $F^rT$, proving injectivity of the
induced map and identifying its image with $I_r$.  A compatible sequence in
the $I_r$ is, component by component, a compatible sequence in every local
quotient; completeness gives an element of the ambient product, and
separatedness makes all face equations hold.  This proves
\eqref{eq:stable-image-limit}.  The last assertion is immediate.
\end{proof}

Levelwise surjectivity of
$\mathfrak g^q/F^{r+1}\to\mathfrak g^q/F^r$ does not by itself imply
surjectivity after taking the end \eqref{eq:tw-end}.  Proposition
\ref{prop:tw-complete} isolates the extra strictness needed to identify every
algebraic finite totalization with a quotient of the completed one.

\begin{theorem}[Filtered and totalized obstruction tower]
\label{thm:total-obstruction}
Let $T=\widehat\TW(\mathfrak g^\bullet)$.  For every $r\geq1$ and every
$a_{r-1}\in\MC(T/F^rT)$ there is a canonical class
\[
 o_r(a_{r-1})\in
 H^2(F^rT/F^{r+1}T,D)
 \tag{3.7}\label{eq:total-obstruction}
\]
whose vanishing is equivalent to a lift in $\MC(T/F^{r+1}T)$.  When lifts
exist, the relative lift and relative gauge-orbit spaces are torsors under
$Z^1(F^rT/F^{r+1}T)$ and $H^1(F^rT/F^{r+1}T)$, respectively.

A compatible sequence
\[
 a_r\in\MC(T/F^{r+1}T),
 \qquad a_r\equiv a_{r-1}\pmod {F^rT},
 \tag{3.8}\label{eq:compatible-tower}
\]
determines a unique element $a\in\MC(T)$.  If the strictness condition
$T/F^rT\cong T_r$ holds, the same assertions may be read directly in the
finite Thom--Whitney totalizations $T_r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite assertions are Theorem~\ref{thm:one-stage} applied to $T$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:tw-complete} gives a unique degree-one element $a\in T$
from \eqref{eq:compatible-tower}.  Its curvature is zero in every quotient,
so
\[
 \Curv(a)\in\bigcap_rF^rT^2=0.
\]
\end{proof}

A natural transformation of semicosimplicial filtered dg Lie algebras
induces a strict filtered map of completed Thom--Whitney carriers.  More
generally, suppose
\[
 P_i:T\longrightarrow R_i\qquad(i\in J)
\]
are filtered dg Lie maps, let
$a_{r-1}\in\MC(T/F^rT)$, and write
\[
 (P_i)_{r-1}:T/F^rT\longrightarrow R_i/F^rR_i
\]
for the induced map.  Naturality of curvature gives
\[
 o_r\bigl((P_i)_{r-1}(a_{r-1})\bigr)=
 H^2(\gr_F^rP_i)\,o_r(a_{r-1}).
 \tag{3.9}\label{eq:row-obstruction}
\]
Hence the vanishing of all projected classes forces the total class to
vanish when
\[
 H^2(\gr_F^rT)\longrightarrow
 \prod_{i\in J}H^2(\gr_F^rR_i)
 \tag{3.10}\label{eq:joint-injectivity}
\]
is injective.  Without this hypothesis, or a direct construction assembling
the projected primitives into a primitive in $T$, the converse fails by
Example~\ref{ex:first}.

\section{Complete filtered \texorpdfstring{$L_\infty$}{L-infinity} algebras}

To keep every degree visible, we now use the shifted symmetric convention.
A flat shifted $L_\infty$ algebra is a graded vector space $L$ with graded
symmetric maps
\[
 q_n:S^nL\longrightarrow L,
 \qquad |q_n|=1,
 \qquad n\geq1,
 \tag{4.1}\label{eq:shifted-brackets}
\]
whose induced coderivation on the reduced symmetric coalgebra squares to
zero.  There is no nullary bracket.  If $L=\mathfrak g[1]$, degree $0$ in
$L$ corresponds to degree $1$ in the unshifted dg Lie algebra
$\mathfrak g$; consequently $H^1(L,q_1)$ corresponds to
$H^2(\mathfrak g,d)$.

Assume
\[
 L=F^1L\supset F^2L\supset\cdots,
 \qquad
 q_n(F^{p_1}L,\ldots,F^{p_n}L)
 \subset F^{p_1+\cdots+p_n}L,
 \qquad
 L\cong\varprojlim_rL/F^rL.
 \tag{4.2}\label{eq:linfty-filtration}
\]
For $a\in L^0$, define
\begin{align}
 R(a)&=\sum_{n\geq1}\frac1{n!}q_n(a^n),
 \tag{4.3}\label{eq:linfty-curvature}\\
 q_m^a(x_1,\ldots,x_m)
 &=\sum_{k\geq0}\frac1{k!}
 q_{m+k}(a^k,x_1,\ldots,x_m).
 \tag{4.4}\label{eq:twist}
\end{align}
The $n$th summand in \eqref{eq:linfty-curvature} lies in $F^nL$, so the
series converges.  Additivity similarly gives convergence of every twisted
bracket.  The $L_\infty$ identities imply
\begin{align}
 q_1^aR(a)&=0,
 \tag{4.5}\label{eq:linfty-bianchi}\\
 R(a+b)&=R(a)+
 \sum_{m\geq1}\frac1{m!}q_m^a(b^m).
 \tag{4.6}\label{eq:linfty-translation}
\end{align}
These formulas and their filtered convergence are developed in
\cite{DolgushevRogers,RogersComplete}.

\begin{proposition}[The $L_\infty$ obstruction]\label{prop:linfty-obstruction}
If $R(a)\in F^rL^1$, then its image defines
\[
 o_r(a)=[R(a)]\in H^1(F^rL/F^{r+1}L,q_1).
 \tag{4.7}\label{eq:shifted-obstruction}
\]
There is $b\in F^rL^0$ with $R(a+b)\in F^{r+1}L$ if and only if
$o_r(a)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $F^r/F^{r+1}$ the operator $q_1^a$ reduces to $q_1$, so
\eqref{eq:linfty-bianchi} makes $R(a)$ a $q_1$-cocycle.  For
$b\in F^rL^0$, every term of \eqref{eq:linfty-translation} except
$q_1b$ lies in $F^{r+1}L$.  Thus
\[
 R(a+b)\equiv R(a)+q_1b\pmod {F^{r+1}L}.
\]
\end{proof}

Without completeness the infinite expression \eqref{eq:linfty-curvature}
need not exist in $L$.  The finite statement remains valid inside
$L/F^{r+1}L$, where additivity makes all but finitely many summands vanish.

A filtered shifted $L_\infty$ morphism $U:L\rightsquigarrow M$ has degree-zero
Taylor coefficients
\[
 U_n:S^nL\longrightarrow M,
 \qquad
 U_n(F^{p_1},\ldots,F^{p_n})\subset F^{p_1+\cdots+p_n}M.
\]
It defines
\[
 U_*(a)=\sum_{n\geq1}\frac1{n!}U_n(a^n),
\]
and satisfies the off-shell curvature identity
\[
 R_M(U_*(a))=
 \sum_{m\geq0}\frac1{m!}
 U_{m+1}(a^m,R_L(a)).
 \tag{4.8}\label{eq:curvature-transport}
\]
Consequently
\[
 o_r(U_*a)=H^1(\gr_F^rU_1)o_r(a).
 \tag{4.9}\label{eq:linfty-naturality}
\]
For a non-strict morphism the right side of
\eqref{eq:curvature-transport} is not merely $U_1R_L(a)$.

There is no canonical exponential gauge group for a general
$L_\infty$ algebra.  Its intrinsic equivalence relation is defined by the
connected components of the completed simplicial Maurer--Cartan set
\[
 \MC_\bullet(L)=
 \MC\!\left(L\widehat\otimes
 \Omega_{\mathrm{poly}}(\Delta^\bullet)\right),
 \qquad
 L\widehat\otimes\Omega_n
 =\varprojlim_r(L/F^rL)\otimes\Omega_n.
 \tag{4.10}\label{eq:mc-simplicial}
\]
For dg Lie algebras this recovers the homotopy theory of
\eqref{eq:gauge-action}; for nilpotent quotients it is the
Deligne--Getzler--Hinich construction \cite{Getzler}.

\section{Comparison morphisms and colored operations}

The Taylor coefficients of a comparison morphism themselves form a complete
filtered carrier.  Let $L$ and $M$ be flat shifted $L_\infty$ algebras, let
$C(L)=\overline S^c(L)$ carry the coderivation $Q_L$, and set
\[
 \mathcal H(L,M)
 =\operatorname{Hom}(C(L),M)
 =\prod_{n\geq1}\operatorname{Hom}(S^nL,M).
 \tag{5.1}\label{eq:comparison-carrier}
\]
Continuous homomorphisms and completed symmetric powers are understood when
$L$ and $M$ have additional complete linear topologies.  The arity filtration
is
\[
 F^p\mathcal H(L,M)
 =\prod_{n\geq p}\operatorname{Hom}(S^nL,M).
 \tag{5.2}\label{eq:comparison-filtration}
\]
If $f$ is homogeneous, the unary bracket is
\[
 q_1^{\mathcal H}(f)=q_1^M\circ f-(-1)^{|f|}f\circ Q_L,
 \tag{5.3}\label{eq:comparison-differential}
\]
and for $m\geq2$ the value
$q_m^{\mathcal H}(h_1,\ldots,h_m)$ is obtained by applying the $m$-fold
reduced coproduct of $C(L)$, then $h_1\otimes\cdots\otimes h_m$ with
Koszul symmetrization, and finally $q_m^M$.  Thus
\[
 q_m^{\mathcal H}(F^{p_1},\ldots,F^{p_m})
 \subset F^{p_1+\cdots+p_m}\mathcal H.
 \tag{5.4}\label{eq:comparison-additivity}
\]
The $L_\infty$ identities follow from $Q_L^2=Q_M^2=0$.  A degree-zero
element $f=(f_1,f_2,\ldots)$ is Maurer--Cartan precisely when the coalgebra
map determined by its Taylor coefficients obeys
\[
 Q_MF=FQ_L;
 \tag{5.5}\label{eq:comparison-equation}
\]
that is, precisely when $F:L\rightsquigarrow M$ is an $L_\infty$ morphism.
This convolution construction is treated systematically in
\cite{RobertNicoudWierstra}.

A chain map alone does not suffice.  For example, let $L$ be the abelian dg
Lie algebra on a degree-one element $x$, and let $M$ have degree-one element
$y$, degree-two central element $z$, zero differential, and
$[y,y]=2z$.  The linear chain map $x\mapsto y$ sends a Maurer--Cartan element
to an element of curvature $z$.  The missing bracket identity is one of the
higher equations in \eqref{eq:comparison-equation}.

There is a parallel carrier for colored algebraic structures.  Let
$\mathcal C$ be a reduced conilpotent coaugmented two-colored dg cooperad with
coaugmentation coideal $\overline{\mathcal C}$.  Reduced means that all
arity-zero terms vanish and that $\overline{\mathcal C}$ has no arity-one
term.  For a two-colored cochain complex $V=(V_\mathrm c,V_\mathrm o)$, define
\[
 \End_V(c_1,\ldots,c_n;c)
 =\operatorname{Hom}
 (V_{c_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{c_n},V_c).
\]
The convolution dg Lie algebra is
\[
 \Conv(\overline{\mathcal C},\End_V)
 =\prod_{\vec c;c}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\Sigma_{\vec c}}
 \bigl(\overline{\mathcal C}(\vec c;c),
       \End_V(\vec c;c)\bigr).
 \tag{5.6}\label{eq:colored-convolution}
\]
For homogeneous $f$ its differential is
\[
 \partial f=d_{\End}f-(-1)^{|f|}f\,d_{\mathcal C}.
 \tag{5.7}\label{eq:convolution-differential}
\]
If $\Delta_{(1)}$ is the infinitesimal cooperadic decomposition and
$\gamma_{(1)}$ is infinitesimal composition in $\End_V$, put
\[
 f\star g=\gamma_{(1)}(f\circ_{(1)}g)\Delta_{(1)},
 \qquad
 [f,g]=f\star g-(-1)^{|f||g|}g\star f.
 \tag{5.8}\label{eq:convolution-bracket}
\]
All permutation and insertion signs in \eqref{eq:convolution-bracket} are
the Koszul signs of the colored symmetric sequences.

There is also a precise filtered topological version.  Suppose each $V_c$ has
a complete separated filtration $G^0V_c=V_c\supset G^1V_c\supset\cdots$
preserved by its differential.  Give an algebraic tensor product the total
filtration
\[
 G^r(V_{c_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{c_n})
 =\sum_{\substack{r_1,\ldots,r_n\geq0\\r_1+\cdots+r_n\geq r}}
 G^{r_1}V_{c_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes G^{r_n}V_{c_n}
\]
and define its completed tensor product as the inverse limit of the
quotients by $G^r$.  Replacing $\operatorname{Hom}$ above by continuous
linear maps gives a topological endomorphism operad provided that
substitution is continuous; this continuity and the associativity of the
completed tensor product are explicit hypotheses.  Under them,
\eqref{eq:convolution-differential}--\eqref{eq:convolution-bracket} extend
continuously.

Suppose $\mathcal C=\bigoplus_{w\geq1}\mathcal C^{(w)}$, its differential
preserves weight, and $\Delta_{(1)}$ splits weight additively.  Maps vanishing
on weights below $p$ form a complete product filtration $W^p$, and
\[
 [W^p,W^q]\subset W^{p+q}.
 \tag{5.9}\label{eq:weight-additivity}
\]
For an arity filtration under operadic insertion the correct positive weight
is arity minus one, since
\[
 (m-1)+(n-1)=(m+n-1)-1.
\]
Thus
\[
 W^p=\{\text{operations of total arity at least }p+1\}
 \tag{5.10}\label{eq:arity-weight}
\]
is additive, whereas filtration by arity at least $p$ satisfies only
$[F^p,F^q]\subset F^{p+q-1}$.  Unary structure must be fixed and absorbed
into the differential, or into a twist by a fixed base Maurer--Cartan
element, before the positive-weight deformation variable is pronilpotent.

Here is the cobar convention compatible with our cohomological grading.  Put
$(E[k])^n=E^{n+k}$, and let
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{coh}}\mathcal C
 =\bigl(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{op}}(\overline{\mathcal C}[-1]),
 d_\Omega\bigr).
 \tag{5.11}\label{eq:cohomological-cobar}
\]
The inclusion of generators
$\kappa:\overline{\mathcal C}\to
\Omega_{\mathrm{coh}}\mathcal C$ has degree $1$.  The cobar differential is
the unique operadic derivation determined by the universal twisting equation;
writing $\gamma^{\mathcal F}_{(1)}$ for infinitesimal composition in the free
colored operad, this equation is
\[
 d_\Omega\kappa(c)
 =-\kappa(d_{\mathcal C}c)
  -\gamma^{\mathcal F}_{(1)}(\kappa\circ_{(1)}\kappa)
    \Delta_{(1)}(c).
 \tag{5.12}\label{eq:cobar-differential}
\]
The cooperad identities give $d_\Omega^2=0$.  A dg operad map
$\Phi:\Omega_{\mathrm{coh}}\mathcal C\to\End_V$ is determined by the
degree-one element $\theta=\Phi\kappa$ and commutes with differentials exactly
when
\[
 \partial\theta+\theta\star\theta
 =\partial\theta+\frac12[\theta,\theta]=0.
 \tag{5.13}\label{eq:cobar-mc}
\]
Thus Maurer--Cartan elements of \eqref{eq:colored-convolution} are precisely
$\Omega_{\mathrm{coh}}\mathcal C$-algebra structures on $V$.  A specified
quasi-free resolution
$\Omega_{\mathrm{coh}}\mathcal C\to\mathcal P$ of a reduced two-colored dg
operad $\mathcal P$ therefore supplies a carrier for homotopy
$\mathcal P$-algebra structures.  An operad cannot be placed in the
cooperadic entry of \eqref{eq:colored-convolution} without such a bar or cobar
construction.  Fixed endpoints may be imposed by twisting the full carrier
by their base structure and taking the kernel of a strict filtered endpoint
map.  If the endpoint map exists only up to coherent homotopy, its specified
$L_\infty$ homotopy fibre is required; an unadorned mapping cone is only a
complex and has no automatic nonlinear brackets.

Assume now that the weight filtration is split and that every bracket has
output weight equal to the sum of its input weights.  Write a positive-weight
deformation as
\[
 \theta=\sum_{p\geq1}\theta^{(p)}.
\]
For a complete filtered $L_\infty$ carrier, the exact weight-$r$ equation is
\[
 q_1\theta^{(r)}+
 \sum_{n=2}^{r}\frac1{n!}
 \sum_{i_1+\cdots+i_n=r}
 q_n(\theta^{(i_1)},\ldots,\theta^{(i_n)})=0.
 \tag{5.14}\label{eq:general-weight-equation}
\]
In the dg Lie case, a closed binary term $\theta^{(1)}$ has the first
curvature
\[
 \Curv(\theta^{(1)})
 \equiv\frac12[\theta^{(1)},\theta^{(1)}]\pmod {W^3},
 \tag{5.15}\label{eq:arity-three-curvature}
\]
and a ternary correction must solve
\[
 d\theta^{(2)}=-\frac12[\theta^{(1)},\theta^{(1)}].
 \tag{5.16}\label{eq:ternary-correction}
\]
This is the universal arity-three obstruction; its value in a particular
geometric theory depends on the specified colored compositions and signs.

\section{Representatives, gauge classes, and derived limits}

Compatible representatives have an elementary limit theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Limit of Maurer--Cartan representatives]
\label{thm:representative-limit}
For an additively filtered dg Lie algebra, the map
\[
 \MC(\mathfrak g)\longrightarrow
 \varprojlim_r\MC(\mathfrak g/F^r\mathfrak g)
 \tag{6.1}\label{eq:mc-limit}
\]
is surjective if
\[
 \mathfrak g^1\longrightarrow
 \varprojlim_r(\mathfrak g/F^r\mathfrak g)^1
\]
is surjective and $\bigcap_rF^r\mathfrak g^2=0$.  It is injective if
$\bigcap_rF^r\mathfrak g^1=0$.  In particular, strict completeness and
separation give a bijection in \eqref{eq:mc-limit}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A compatible family on the right has a degree-one lift $a\in\mathfrak g^1$
under the stated surjectivity.  Its curvature has zero image in every
quotient, so degree-two separation gives $\Curv(a)=0$.  Degree-one separation
gives uniqueness.  No derived-limit term occurs because the representatives
were already compatible.
\end{proof}

The same proof applies to \eqref{eq:linfty-curvature} under strict
completeness and the additive convergence hypotheses.  Vanishing of every
$H^2(A_r)$ is a convenient sufficient condition for constructing a
compatible dg Lie sequence recursively from the zero element in
$\mathfrak g/F^1\mathfrak g$.  The mere nonemptiness of each finite solution
set does not choose such a sequence.

Gauge classes obey a homotopical limit theorem instead.  Assume for the
remainder of this paragraph that $\mathfrak g$ is strictly complete and
separated, and put
\[
 X_r=\MC_\bullet(\mathfrak g/F^r\mathfrak g),
 \qquad
 X=\MC_\bullet(\mathfrak g)=\varprojlim_rX_r,
 \tag{6.2}\label{eq:tower-mc-spaces}
\]
where the tensor products in $X$ are completed.  The $X_r$ are Kan complexes,
and the maps $X_{r+1}\to X_r$ are Kan fibrations.  For a compatible
basepoint $a=(a_r)$, the Milnor sequence for a tower of fibrations gives an
exact sequence of pointed sets
\[
 *\longrightarrow
 \varprojlim\nolimits^1_r\pi_1(X_r,a_r)
 \longrightarrow
 \pi_0(X)
 \longrightarrow
 \varprojlim_r\pi_0(X_r)
 \longrightarrow *.
 \tag{6.3}\label{eq:gauge-milnor}
\]
Here $\varprojlim^1$ is the nonabelian orbit-set construction for a tower of
groups.  The group $\pi_1(X_r,a_r)$ is the derived stabilizer; in dg Lie
terms it is $H^0((\mathfrak g/F^r)^{a_r})$ with its Campbell--Hausdorff group
law.  Thus compatible finite-stage gauge classes have a global class, but
they determine it uniquely only when the pointed set
\[
 \varprojlim\nolimits^1_r\pi_1(X_r,a_r)
 \tag{6.4}\label{eq:stabilizer-lim1}
\]
is trivial.  A Mittag--Leffler stabilizer tower, in particular a tower with
surjective transition maps, is a standard sufficient condition.  The exact
sequence \eqref{eq:gauge-milnor} is the degree-zero case of the homotopy-limit
sequence for towers of fibrations \cite{Hirschhorn}.

There is a related, but distinct, $\varprojlim^1$ for compatible primitives.
For a countable inverse system of cochain complexes $(C_r,p_r)$, let
\[
 s:\prod_rC_r\longrightarrow\prod_rC_r,
 \qquad
 s((c_r))=(p_r(c_{r+1})).
\]
Then
\[
 \Rlim C_r\simeq
 \Cone\!\left(1-s:\prod_rC_r\longrightarrow\prod_rC_r\right)[-1]
 \tag{6.5}\label{eq:derived-limit-cone}
\]
and there is a natural exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow
 \varprojlim\nolimits^1_rH^{j-1}(C_r)
 \longrightarrow H^j(\Rlim C_r)
 \longrightarrow\varprojlim_rH^j(C_r)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{6.6}\label{eq:cohomology-milnor}
\]
Indeed, products are exact in vector spaces, so
$H^j(\prod_rC_r)=\prod_rH^j(C_r)$.  The long exact cohomology sequence of
\eqref{eq:derived-limit-cone} identifies its two adjacent terms with the
cokernel of $1-s$ on $\prod_rH^{j-1}(C_r)$ and the kernel of $1-s$ on
$\prod_rH^j(C_r)$, which are respectively $\varprojlim^1H^{j-1}(C_r)$ and
$\varprojlim H^j(C_r)$.
When the transition maps are degreewise surjective, the ordinary inverse
limit represents \eqref{eq:derived-limit-cone}.  This does not force the
left term of \eqref{eq:cohomology-milnor} to vanish, because the induced
cohomology maps need not be Mittag--Leffler.

Under this degreewise-surjectivity hypothesis, suppose compatible cocycles
$c_r\in Z^j(C_r)$ are exact at every stage.
Choose $b_r$ with $db_r=-c_r$.  Then
\[
 \delta_r=b_r-p_r(b_{r+1})\in Z^{j-1}(C_r)
 \tag{6.7}\label{eq:primitive-defect}
\]
represents the residual class in
$\varprojlim^1H^{j-1}(C_r)$.  Compatible primitives exist precisely when the
single class of $(c_r)$ in $H^j(\Rlim C_r)$ vanishes; after stagewise
vanishing, this is equivalent to the vanishing of the residual
\eqref{eq:primitive-defect}.  There is no natural decomposition of this
class as an element of
\[
 \varprojlim H^j(C_r)\oplus
 \varprojlim\nolimits^1H^{j-1}(C_r)
\]
without a chosen splitting of \eqref{eq:cohomology-milnor}.  For ordinary
Maurer--Cartan curvature $j=2$, so the residual primitive group is
$\varprojlim^1H^1$, not $\varprojlim^1H^0$.
Without a quasi-isomorphism
$\varprojlim C_r\to\Rlim C_r$, derived vanishing controls a
homotopy-coherent primitive and need not produce a strictly compatible one.

\section{Sharp failures}

\begin{example}[Separated but incomplete]\label{ex:incomplete}
Let $\mathfrak a^1=t\Bbbk[t]$, with zero differential and bracket, and
$F^r\mathfrak a^1=t^r\Bbbk[t]$.  The compatible elements
\[
 a_r=t+t^2+\cdots+t^{r-1}
 \in\mathfrak a/F^r\mathfrak a
\]
are Maurer--Cartan.  No element of $t\Bbbk[t]$ represents their limit
$\sum_{j\geq1}t^j$.  Separation without completeness does not give
surjectivity in \eqref{eq:mc-limit}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Complete quotients with hidden curvature]\label{ex:nonsep}
Use the dg Lie algebra of Example~\ref{ex:first}, but put
\[
 F^1\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g,
 \qquad
 F^r\mathfrak g=\Bbbk z\quad(r\geq2).
\]
Every quotient sees $x$ as Maurer--Cartan, while
$\Curv(x)=z\neq0$.  The curvature lies in
$\bigcap_rF^r\mathfrak g^2$.  Degree-two separation in
Theorem~\ref{thm:representative-limit} is therefore necessary.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A non-Mittag--Leffler cohomology tower]
\label{ex:nonml}
Let
\[
 C_r=\left[
 \Bbbk[x]\xrightarrow{\bmod x^r}\Bbbk[x]/(x^r)
 \right]
\]
in degrees $0$ and $1$, with transition maps the identity in degree $0$ and
the quotient in degree $1$.  These maps are degreewise surjective, but
\[
 H^0(C_r)=(x^r),
 \qquad H^1(C_r)=0,
\]
and the maps on $H^0$ are the proper inclusions
$(x^{r+1})\hookrightarrow(x^r)$.  Hence
\[
 \varprojlim_rC_r=
 \left[\Bbbk[x]\hookrightarrow\Bbbk[[x]]\right],
 \qquad
 H^1(\varprojlim C_r)=\Bbbk[[x]]/\Bbbk[x]
 \cong\varprojlim\nolimits^1_r(x^r).
 \tag{7.1}\label{eq:nonml-calculation}
\]
The compatible degree-one cocycles
\[
 1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1}\pmod{x^r}
\]
are exact separately, but their formal-series limit has no polynomial
primitive.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Finite gauge classes do not determine the total class]
\label{ex:gauge-lim1}
Regard
\[
 \mathfrak a^0=\Bbbk[x],
 \qquad
 \mathfrak a^1=\Bbbk[[x]],
 \qquad
 d(f)=f,
 \qquad [-,-]=0
\]
as an abelian dg Lie algebra, and set, for $r\geq2$,
\[
 F^r\mathfrak a^0=0,
 \qquad
 F^r\mathfrak a^1=x^{r-1}\Bbbk[[x]],
 \qquad F^1\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a.
\]
It is strictly complete and separated.  In every finite quotient the map
$d:\mathfrak a^0\to\mathfrak a^1/F^r$ is surjective, so there is one gauge
class.  Globally,
\[
 \MC(\mathfrak a)/\text{gauge}
 =H^1(\mathfrak a)
 =\Bbbk[[x]]/\Bbbk[x].
 \tag{7.2}\label{eq:gauge-counterexample}
\]
This is precisely the $\varprojlim^1$ ambiguity in
\eqref{eq:gauge-milnor}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Stagewise solvability in a curved algebra]
\label{ex:curved}
Let $K^1=\Bbbk[t]$, $K^2=\Bbbk[[t]]$, let $d:K^1\to K^2$ be inclusion,
and let the bracket vanish.  Give this curved abelian dg Lie algebra the
curvature
\[
 \omega=\sum_{j\geq0}t^j
\]
and filtration
\[
 F^1K=K,
 \qquad
 F^rK^1=0,
 \qquad
 F^rK^2=t^{r-1}\Bbbk[[t]]\quad(r\geq2).
\]
The filtered complex is strictly complete.  In $K/F^rK$, the curved
Maurer--Cartan equation $\omega+da=0$ has the solution
\[
 a_r=-(1+t+\cdots+t^{r-2}).
\]
There is no compatible family of solutions: compatibility would force one
polynomial to agree with $-\omega$ to every order.  Thus the nonemptiness of
every finite solution set does
not replace a compatible sequence.  More fundamentally, a nullary bracket in a
curved $L_\infty$ algebra makes $q_1^2$ contain a bracket with $q_0$; the
cohomology group in \eqref{eq:shifted-obstruction} is then unavailable unless
additional filtration hypotheses make the associated-graded unary operator
square to zero.  The main theorems are therefore flat.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Completeness without additive convergence]
Let $L^0=\Bbbk x$, $L^1=\Bbbk z$, and define shifted brackets by
\[
 q_n(x^n)=n!z\quad(n\geq2),
\]
with every bracket containing $z$ equal to zero.  All $L_\infty$ identities
hold because every composite bracket vanishes.  The discrete filtration
$F^1L=L$, $F^2L=0$ is complete and separated, but it is not additive for the
higher brackets.  The series $R(x)=\sum_{n\geq2}z$ does not converge.  In the
same way, $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\Bbbk)[[t]]$ with
$F^1\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g$ and
$F^r\mathfrak g=t^{r-1}\mathfrak g$ for $r\geq2$ is complete but not
additively filtered: a constant adjoint action need not converge in the
$t$-adic topology.  Completeness is useful only after the operations have
been shown to move terms deeper.
\end{example}

\section{Mathematical scope}

The preceding results apply to a mixed holomorphic--topological deformation
problem once the following mathematical data are supplied:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item a semicosimplicial diagram of complete local deformation dg Lie
 algebras with strict filtration-preserving cofaces, or a separately
 specified complete filtered $L_\infty$ homotopy-limit carrier;
 \item a positive additive filtration and completed tensor products for all
 infinite operation spaces;
 \item full filtered $L_\infty$ comparison morphisms, including their higher
 Taylor coefficients;
 \item for an operadic model, a reduced conilpotent colored cooperad and a
 specified quasi-free resolution, together with its continuous colored
 endomorphism object;
 \item a flat total carrier with zero nullary bracket.  Additional BV or
 central terms must be incorporated into a square-zero unary operator and
 compatible higher brackets.  A genuinely curved carrier is covered only
 after twisting by a specified Maurer--Cartan basepoint makes its nullary
 bracket vanish and preserves the additive filtration.
\end{enumerate}
An arbitrary chain of deformation complexes is not a totalization: its
diagram, homotopy coherences, cross-operations, and total differential must
be given.  Likewise, projected obstruction classes are consequences of the
total class, not coordinates of it unless joint detection such as
\eqref{eq:joint-injectivity} is proved.

For the dg Lie alternative in item 1, Theorem~\ref{thm:total-obstruction} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:representative-limit} give the filtered-and-totalized
obstruction tower.  For a separately specified complete flat
$L_\infty$ carrier, Proposition~\ref{prop:linfty-obstruction} gives its
successive obstruction classes and the same completeness argument gives the
limit of compatible representatives.  These results do not by themselves
construct a particular mixed holomorphic--topological colored carrier or
evaluate its arity-three class; that value is the explicit composition
\eqref{eq:arity-three-curvature} in the chosen chain-level model.
